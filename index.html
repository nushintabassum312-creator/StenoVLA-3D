Abstract

3D-aware reasoning for stenosis traversal

Autonomous endoscopic navigation requires a policy model to predict actions from texture-poor monocular observations, make safe control decisions, and retain evidence of lesions after they leave the field of view. Existing vision-language-action models primarily rely on visual appearance and short-term context, limiting geometric grounding and episode-level reporting.

StenoVLA-3D integrates point maps into the Cosmos-Reason 2 backbone through learned geometry-gated fusion and introduces a temporal state branch to model traversal progress. Its reasoning-and-action backbone predicts grounded reasoning with actions, while dedicated heads estimate stenosis shape and generate the final lesion report. We also introduce EndoCausal, an episode-level dataset with lesion annotations, actions, and temporally grounded reasoning.

On 40 held-out recorded test episodes, StenoVLA-3D reaches 95.2% semantic accuracy and 83.4% action accuracy. On a physical 3-DoF endoscope, it attains 88.9% and 77.8% task success in esophageal and colonic phantoms, respectively, across 36 trials per procedure.

Core idea

Reasoning, geometry, and memory in one VLA

Unlike direct-action and QA-based systems, StenoVLA-3D adds explicit numeric geometry, persistent lesion-event memory, and a shared reasoning-and-action head.

Approach

A geometry-conditioned VLA with dual-timescale memory

01

3D geometry fusion

DA3 point maps are compressed into a geometry embedding and injected through a learned feature-wise gate without changing the pretrained token sequence.

02

Temporal traversal memory

Recent observations support lumen alignment and distinguish approach, entry, traversal, and completion across changing views.

03

Persistent lesion memory

Compact lesion-event evidence is retained after lesions leave the field of view and is reused for episode-level reporting.

04

Structured control

The shared backbone produces reasoning and discrete 3-DoF actions, while navigation state gates asynchronous reconstruction.

Qualitative behavior

Navigation across the complete stenosis workflow

Representative esophagus and colon sequences show lesion detection, contact avoidance, stenosis alignment, reconstruction-gated entry, traversal, and distal-end stopping.

Strong open-loop prediction and physical navigation
